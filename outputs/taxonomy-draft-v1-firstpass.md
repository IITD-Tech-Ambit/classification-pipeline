# Research Ambit Taxonomy Draft v2 — For Team Review

_Drafted 2026-07-17 from the live paper corpus (70,298 papers in `researchmetadatascopus`). Embeddings: `BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5`; clustering: KMeans with silhouette-based k selection; naming/definitions: `claude-haiku-4-5`._

## Rules of the taxonomy

- **Two levels**: 9 fixed thematic areas (names unchanged from v1) → 5–10 domains each. The old shared 35-domain pool and 177 subdomains are retired; each domain below belongs to exactly one theme.
- **Single label**: every paper gets exactly one theme and one domain within it.
- **No overlaps**: domain definitions within a theme are mutually exclusive; cross-theme boundary calls are settled by the tie-break rules embedded in each scope note.
- Domain lists below were derived by clustering actual paper samples (~500/theme), so estimated shares reflect the sampled corpus, not aspiration.

## AI/ML, Supercomputing & Quantum Computing

_v1-assigned papers: 5,259 · sampled: 550 · clusters found: 8 · domains: 6_

**Scope.** This theme encompasses computational and algorithmic foundations for artificial intelligence, machine learning, and high-performance computing, including quantum computing theory and algorithms. It covers quantum algorithms, quantum information processing, and quantum error correction; classical and parallel algorithms for computational geometry, graph problems, and distributed systems; neural network architectures, deep learning methods, and physics-informed machine learning; natural language processing, semantic analysis, and information retrieval on text and network data; plasma physics simulations and computational fluid dynamics; and machine learning applications to materials science and physics for discovering property relationships and building interpretable models. Excluded are quantum device fabrication, quantum materials physics, and semiconductor engineering, which belong to Quantum Technologies & Semiconductor Technology; biomaterial synthesis for clinical applications, which belongs to Healthcare & MedTech; and energy generation, storage, and conversion technologies, which belong to Energy, Sustainability & Climate Change. The key tie-breaker with Quantum Technologies is algorithmic: papers on quantum algorithms, quantum computation, and quantum information processing belong here, while work on fabricating quantum devices, quantum dots, or developing quantum materials goes to the Quantum Technologies theme. Similarly, machine learning contributions are assigned here based on methodological novelty, whereas AI methods applied to communication systems or networks are assigned to Next-Gen Communication only if the system contribution (not the algorithm) is primary. Materials informatics research belongs here when the focus is on ML-driven discovery of material properties; if the focus is synthesis or novel material characterization without learning components, it goes to Advanced Materials & Devices.

## Advanced Materials & Devices

_v1-assigned papers: 10,372 · sampled: 550 · clusters found: 6 · domains: 6_

**Scope.** Advanced Materials & Devices encompasses the discovery, synthesis, characterization, and fundamental structure-property relationships of novel materials and engineered material systems across six complementary domains: plasmonic nanostructures and optical materials (metallic nanoparticles for photonics and sensing); polymer nanocomposites with carbon-based fillers for enhanced functional properties; biomaterials and bioactive compounds; periodic metastructures and metamaterials for wave engineering; organic molecules and coordination complexes with photonic and optoelectronic functionality; and ferroelectric and multiferroic ceramics. The theme focuses on materials science, material property enhancement, and device-level material engineering—not on system integration, application-specific device optimization, or commercialization. Biomaterials designed for specific clinical or therapeutic applications (e.g., drug delivery systems, implants for tissue engineering with clinical intent) belong in Healthcare & MedTech; general biomaterial synthesis and bioactivity characterization without clinical specification remain here. Processing, joining, machining, or scale-up of materials for manufacturing goes to Manufacturing & Industry 4.0, while fundamental discovery and characterization stays here. Quantum devices, semiconductor fabrication, or quantum material systems go to Quantum Technologies & Semiconductor Technology; if a paper's primary contribution is a new algorithmic approach or computational method applied to materials (even for property prediction), route it to AI/ML, Supercomputing & Quantum Computing. Papers on catalytic materials for clean energy conversion or energy storage belong in Energy, Sustainability & Climate Change; those on structural materials and sustainability in buildings belong in Smart & Sustainable Infrastructure.

## Energy, Sustainability & Climate Change

_v1-assigned papers: 14,031 · sampled: 550 · clusters found: 7 · domains: 6_

**Scope.** This theme encompasses research on renewable energy systems, energy storage technologies, thermal efficiency, and atmospheric science in the service of climate mitigation and sustainable energy transitions. It includes grid integration of solar and wind systems with storage and microgrids; battery design and electrochemical optimization for stationary and mobile applications; techno-economic feasibility and policy analysis for renewable energy deployment; catalytic pathways for sustainable fuels and carbon utilization; thermodynamic optimization of solar thermal and heat recovery systems; and monitoring and modeling of atmospheric pollutants and greenhouse gases affecting India and Asia. Research should focus on the energy conversion, storage, utilization, or environmental characterization itself—not on the broader infrastructure or policy context in isolation. Papers addressing energy-efficient buildings, smart grid distribution networks as infrastructure systems, or urban energy planning belong instead in Smart & Sustainable Infrastructure; papers on general battery material synthesis without performance modeling for energy applications, or on materials discovery in catalysis, belong in Advanced Materials & Devices; pure economics or policy analysis disconnected from technology assessment belongs in Social Sciences, Humanities & Management. A paper on floating solar feasibility with techno-economic modeling stays here; one on grid modernization or demand-side management in cities goes to Smart Infrastructure. A paper optimizing Li-ion cell chemistry and cycling performance stays here; one on synthesizing a new nanostructured cathode material without energy application context goes to Advanced Materials. Catalysis papers on CO₂ reduction or biomass conversion for fuels and chemicals remain in this theme; catalysis for other industrial or pharmaceutical applications goes to Manufacturing or Healthcare.

## Healthcare & MedTech

_v1-assigned papers: 7,720 · sampled: 550 · clusters found: 14 · domains: 11_

**Scope.** Healthcare & MedTech encompasses translational and applied research aimed at disease diagnosis, treatment, and monitoring through biomedical innovation. This includes infectious disease research (viral/bacterial detection and drug repurposing), therapeutic delivery systems and tissue engineering, protein and molecular engineering for biomedical applications, biopharmaceutical manufacturing and quality control, medical devices and wearable sensors, diagnostic imaging and sensing platforms, cancer biology and molecular oncology, and the application of AI/ML to clinical decision-making and diagnosis. The theme covers work spanning from molecular mechanisms and computational biomechanics through clinical implementation, as exemplified by papers on SARS-CoV-2 surveillance, drug carrier design, protein aggregation studies, biosimilar characterization, wearable motion capture, and quantitative tissue imaging. Excluded from this theme: general biomaterial synthesis and characterization without a specific clinical/therapeutic endpoint (assign to Advanced Materials & Devices); machine learning methodology papers where the contribution is the algorithm itself rather than its clinical application (assign to AI/ML, Supercomputing & Quantum Computing); quantum computing and quantum information processing (assign to AI/ML theme); semiconductor device physics and fabrication for non-biomedical applications (assign to Quantum Technologies & Semiconductor Technology); and bioprocess manufacturing at scale for non-therapeutic purposes (assign to Manufacturing & Industry 4.0). Operationally, if a paper develops a biomaterial, drug delivery carrier, or diagnostic device specifically for a therapeutic or clinical application, assign it to Healthcare & MedTech; if the paper's primary contribution is the biomaterial synthesis or characterization method itself without clinical context, assign to Advanced Materials. If a paper applies a known AI/ML algorithm to clinical diagnosis or treatment planning, assign to Healthcare & MedTech; if the paper's contribution is advancing the ML methodology, assign to AI/ML.

## Manufacturing & Industry 4.0

## Next-Gen Communication

This includes antenna and RF component design across microwave and millimeter-wave frequencies; theoretical analysis and optimization of multi-antenna systems, beamforming, and relay networks; electromagnetic wave interactions in plasma media; medium access control, channel access, and resource allocation in wireless networks (IoT, cellular, ad hoc); and signal processing, filtering, and wavelet methods applied specifically to wireless transmission and reception. Papers in this theme typically focus on optimizing communication system performance, improving spectral efficiency, designing hardware components, or analyzing physical propagation phenomena. Research on general signal processing methodology, machine learning algorithms, or AI methods should be assigned to the AI/ML theme, even when applied to networks—the Next-Gen Communication theme receives contributions where the novelty lies in the communication system or signal transmission itself, not the algorithmic method. Quantum devices, semiconductor fabrication, and photonic device physics belong in Quantum Technologies & Semiconductor Technology. Biomaterials or drug delivery systems for clinical applications belong in Healthcare & MedTech. ## Quantum Technologies & Semiconductor Technology

_v1-assigned papers: 3,540 · sampled: 550 · clusters found: 14 · domains: 6_

**Scope.** This theme encompasses the design, fabrication, characterization, and physics of quantum devices, semiconductors, and photonic systems—covering quantum optics and photonic quantum materials, semiconductor thin films and nanostructures, nanoscale transistor engineering, quantum dots and nanocrystals, magnetic materials and spintronics, and photonic sensing platforms. Research here focuses on material synthesis and device engineering, electrical and optical property characterization, and the demonstration of functional devices such as photodetectors, sensors, and memory elements. Explicitly excluded are quantum algorithms, quantum information processing, and quantum computing frameworks, which belong to the AI/ML, Supercomputing & Quantum Computing theme; the boundary is that quantum algorithms and quantum computing *software* go to AI/ML while quantum *devices* and quantum *materials* stay here. Also excluded are biomaterials developed for specific clinical applications (which go to Healthcare & MedTech), general-purpose plasmonic nanostructures studied primarily for optical materials science (which go to Advanced Materials & Devices), and energy harvesting or storage devices viewed primarily as energy conversion systems (which go to the Energy theme); by contrast, quantum dot photovoltaics, photodetectors, and colloidal nanocrystal light emitters belong here because the focus is device physics and material properties rather than energy system performance. Papers studying firm internationalization, poverty and inequality, welfare program implementation, Nash equilibrium, auction mechanisms, and dependency parsing for Indian languages all belong here. Education policy, design studies, and pure humanities work also belong here. The theme serves as the disciplinary home for social science, management, and humanities research distinct from STEM-focused technological or scientific advancement.
